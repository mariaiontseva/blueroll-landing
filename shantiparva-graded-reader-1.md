# Śāntiparva — Graded Reader 1: the origin of kingship (matsya-nyāya)

**What this is.** A self-contained reading drill in three layers of
decreasing help. Read Layer A line by line, then cover it and attempt
Layer B, then cover that and translate Layer C cold. Re-read the whole
thing 3–4 times across the week — the gains come from *re-reading*, not
from chasing new passages.

> **Honesty note.** These six ślokas are **constructed practice verses**
> in authentic epic *anuṣṭubh* on the canonical Rājadharma theme (the
> kingless world, the law of the fish, the creation of the rod). They
> are grammatically real epic Sanskrit but are **not a verbatim
> quotation** — do **not** cite them on an exam as a numbered Mahābhārata
> verse. For the genuine passage on this exact theme, read **MBh
> 12.67** (Bhīṣma to Yudhiṣṭhira on the origin of *daṇḍa*) via GRETIL
> + Ganguli's translation. The vocabulary, syntax, and formulas here
> are exactly what you will meet there.

---

## How to read each verse (the algorithm)

1. Split into four *pādas* (~8 syllables each).
2. Find the finite verb (often verse-final).
3. Find the subject (nominative).
4. Bracket compounds, name the type.
5. Untangle correlatives (`yasmin … tatra`, etc.).
6. Translate pāda by pāda, then assemble.

For each verse: (1) text with sandhi, (2) *padapāṭha* (words separated /

### Verse 1

**Text (with sandhi):**
> purā na rājā nāsīc ca daṇḍo naiva ca daṇḍadhṛk |
> dharmeṇaiva prajāḥ sarvā rakṣanti sma parasparam ||

**Padapāṭha (resolved):**
> purā · na · rājā · na · āsīt · ca · daṇḍaḥ · na · eva · ca · daṇḍa-dhṛk |
> dharmeṇa · eva · prajāḥ · sarvāḥ · rakṣanti · sma · parasparam |

| Word | Analysis | Meaning |
|------|----------|---------|
| purā | indeclinable | formerly, of old |
| na | indecl. | not |
| rājā | rājan, m., nom. sg. | king |
| (na) āsīt | √as, imperfect 3 sg. | there was (not) |
| ca | indecl. | and |
| daṇḍaḥ | daṇḍa, m., nom. sg. | the rod (punishment) |
| na eva ca | = naiva ca | nor … either |
| daṇḍa-dhṛk | daṇḍa-dhṛj, m. nom. sg., tatpuruṣa | rod-bearer (= one who punishes) |
| dharmeṇa | dharma, m., instr. sg. | by dharma |
| eva | indecl. | alone, just |
| prajāḥ | prajā, f., nom. pl. | subjects, people |
| sarvāḥ | sarva, f. nom. pl. | all |
| rakṣanti sma | √rakṣ, pres. 3 pl. + sma | used to protect (past habitual) |
| parasparam | adv. | one another |

> **Note:** `rakṣanti sma` — *present + sma* = past habitual ("used to
> protect"). A very common epic device; memorize it.

**Literal:** "Formerly there-was no king, and no rod, nor rod-bearer
either; by-dharma alone subjects all used-to-protect one-another."

**Idiomatic:** *In olden times there was no king, no rod of punishment,
and no one to wield it; all the people protected one another by dharma
alone.*

### Verse 2

**Text (with sandhi):**
> tato lobhābhibhūtānāṃ naṣṭe dharme parasparam |
> balavān durbalaṃ hanti matsyo matsyam ivāmbhasi ||

**Padapāṭha:**
> tataḥ · lobha-abhibhūtānām · naṣṭe · dharme · parasparam |
> balavān · durbalam · hanti · matsyaḥ · matsyam · iva · ambhasi |

| Word | Analysis | Meaning |
|------|----------|---------|
| tataḥ | adv. | then, thereupon |
| lobha-abhibhūtānām | gen. pl. m., tatpuruṣa | of those overcome by greed |
| naṣṭe dharme | **locative absolute** (ppl. √naś + dharma, loc. sg.) | dharma being destroyed / when dharma was lost |
| parasparam | adv. | mutually |
| balavān | balavat, m. nom. sg. | the strong (one) |
| durbalam | acc. sg. m. | the weak (one) |
| hanti | √han, pres. 3 sg. | kills, strikes |
| matsyaḥ | m. nom. sg. | a fish |
| matsyam | m. acc. sg. | a fish (obj.) |
| iva | indecl. | like, as |
| ambhasi | ambhas, n. loc. sg. | in the water |

> **Note:** `naṣṭe dharme` is the key construction — a **locative
> absolute**: noun + participle both in the locative = "when/because X
> happened." Don't translate the locatives literally.

**Literal:** "Then, of-those-overcome-by-greed, dharma-being-destroyed,
mutually, the-strong the-weak kills, like a-fish a-fish in-the-water."

**Idiomatic:** *Then, when dharma was destroyed among men overcome by
greed, the strong began to kill the weak, as a big fish devours a small
one in the water.* — This is the **matsya-nyāya**, the "law of the
fish."

### Verse 3

**Text (with sandhi):**
> te 'bhigamya tato devaṃ brahmāṇaṃ lokasaṃbhavam |
> ūcuḥ prāñjalayaḥ sarve pīḍyamānāḥ parasparam ||

**Padapāṭha:**
> te · abhigamya · tataḥ · devam · brahmāṇam · loka-saṃbhavam |
> ūcuḥ · prāñjalayaḥ · sarve · pīḍyamānāḥ · parasparam |

| Word | Analysis | Meaning |
|------|----------|---------|
| te | tad, m. nom. pl. | they |
| abhigamya | abhi+√gam, **absolutive** | having approached |
| tataḥ | adv. | then |
| devam | m. acc. sg. | the god |
| brahmāṇam | brahman, m. acc. sg. | Brahmā |
| loka-saṃbhavam | acc. sg., tatpuruṣa | source of the world |
| ūcuḥ | √vac, **perfect 3 pl.** | they said |
| prāñjalayaḥ | nom. pl., bahuvrīhi | with palms joined |
| sarve | sarva, m. nom. pl. | all |
| pīḍyamānāḥ | passive pres. ppl., nom. pl. | being oppressed |
| parasparam | adv. | by one another |

> **Note:** `abhigamya` = absolutive ("having approached"); `ūcuḥ` =
> epic perfect of √vac ("they said") — one of your top-25 must-know
> perfects. `prāñjalayaḥ` is a bahuvrīhi: "(those) of-whom palms (are)
> joined" = "with joined palms."

**Literal:** "They, having-approached then the-god Brahmā
world-source, said, palms-joined, all, being-oppressed by-one-another."

**Idiomatic:** *Then they all, oppressed by one another, approached the
god Brahmā, source of the world, and with joined palms said:*

### Verse 4 (direct speech)

**Text (with sandhi):**
> anāthāḥ smo vinaśyāmo rakṣitā no na vidyate |
> tasmāt kuruṣva naḥ kaṃcit prabhuṃ rakṣārtham añjasā ||

**Padapāṭha:**
> anāthāḥ · smaḥ · vinaśyāmaḥ · rakṣitā · naḥ · na · vidyate |
> tasmāt · kuruṣva · naḥ · kaṃcit · prabhum · rakṣā-artham · añjasā |

| Word | Analysis | Meaning |
|------|----------|---------|
| anāthāḥ | nom. pl. m. | lordless, without protector |
| smaḥ | √as, pres. 1 pl. | we are |
| vinaśyāmaḥ | vi+√naś, pres. 1 pl. | we perish |
| rakṣitā | agent noun, m. nom. sg. | a protector |
| naḥ | encl. gen. pl. | of us, our |
| na vidyate | √vid, pass. 3 sg. | is not found, does not exist |
| tasmāt | abl. → adv. | therefore |
| kuruṣva | √kṛ, imv. 2 sg. ātm. | make! |
| naḥ | encl. dat./gen. pl. | for us |
| kaṃcit | acc. sg. m. | someone, some |
| prabhum | m. acc. sg. | lord, master |
| rakṣā-artham | acc. → adv., tatpuruṣa | for the sake of protection |
| añjasā | adv. (instr.) | straightway, at once |

> **Note:** `smaḥ`, `vinaśyāmaḥ` — first-person plurals; spotting the
> `-maḥ` ending tells you "we." `kuruṣva` is the ātmanepada imperative
> of √kṛ — "make!" `-artham` at the end of a compound = "for the sake
> of X" (turns the compound into a purpose adverb).

**Literal:** "Lordless we-are, we-perish; a-protector of-us not
is-found. Therefore make for-us some lord, for-protection's-sake, at
once."

**Idiomatic:** *"We are without a protector and we are perishing; we
have no one to guard us. Therefore appoint for us at once some lord, for
our protection."*

### Verse 5

**Text (with sandhi):**
> tataḥ sa bhagavān devo manuṃ rājānam ādiśat |
> daṇḍaṃ ca sṛṣṭavāñ chakto bhūtānāṃ hitakāmyayā ||

**Padapāṭha:**
> tataḥ · saḥ · bhagavān · devaḥ · manum · rājānam · ādiśat |
> daṇḍam · ca · sṛṣṭavān · śaktaḥ · bhūtānām · hita-kāmyayā |

| Word | Analysis | Meaning |
|------|----------|---------|
| tataḥ | adv. | then |
| saḥ | tad, m. nom. sg. | he, that |
| bhagavān | nom. sg. | the blessed (one) |
| devaḥ | m. nom. sg. | god |
| manum | m. acc. sg. | Manu |
| rājānam | rājan, m. acc. sg. | (as) king |
| ādiśat | ā+√diś, imperfect 3 sg. | appointed, ordained |
| daṇḍam | m. acc. sg. | the rod |
| ca | and | |
| sṛṣṭavān | perf. active ppl., nom. sg. | (he) created |
| śaktaḥ | nom. sg. | able, capable |
| bhūtānām | bhūta, n. gen. pl. | of beings |
| hita-kāmyayā | f. instr. sg., tatpuruṣa | out of desire for (their) welfare |

> **Note:** `manuṃ rājānam ādiśat` — **double accusative**: "appointed
> Manu (as) king." `sṛṣṭavān` is a perfect active participle used as a
> finite past ("he created"). Sandhi: `sṛṣṭavān + śaktaḥ → sṛṣṭavāñ
> chaktaḥ`.

**Literal:** "Then he, the-blessed god, Manu (as)-king appointed; the-rod
and created, able, of-beings out-of-desire-for-welfare."

**Idiomatic:** *Then the blessed god appointed Manu as king, and, able
(to do so), he created the rod, out of a desire for the welfare of all
beings.*

### Verse 6 (gnomic conclusion)

**Text (with sandhi):**
> yasmin rāṣṭre sunītena daṇḍena pālyate prajā |
> tatra dharmaḥ sthiro nityaṃ matsyanyāyo na jāyate ||

**Padapāṭha:**
> yasmin · rāṣṭre · su-nītena · daṇḍena · pālyate · prajā |
> tatra · dharmaḥ · sthiraḥ · nityam · matsya-nyāyaḥ · na · jāyate |

| Word | Analysis | Meaning |
|------|----------|---------|
| yasmin | yad, m. loc. sg. | in which |
| rāṣṭre | rāṣṭra, n. loc. sg. | kingdom |
| su-nītena | instr. sg., karmadhāraya | well-applied, justly-guided |
| daṇḍena | daṇḍa, m. instr. sg. | by the rod |
| pālyate | √pāl, pass. 3 sg. | is protected |
| prajā | f. nom. sg. | the populace |
| tatra | adv. | there |
| dharmaḥ | m. nom. sg. | dharma |
| sthiraḥ | nom. sg. | firm, steady |
| nityam | adv. | always |
| matsya-nyāyaḥ | m. nom. sg., tatpuruṣa | the law of the fish |
| na jāyate | √jan, pres. mid. 3 sg. | does not arise |

> **Note:** the **correlative** `yasmin rāṣṭre … tatra` = "in which
> kingdom … there." Relative clause first, main clause second — flip
> for English. `pālyate`, `jāyate` are passives/middles in `-yate`.

**Literal:** "In-which kingdom by-well-applied rod is-protected
the-populace, there dharma firm always, the-law-of-the-fish not
arises."

**Idiomatic:** *In whatever kingdom the people are protected by a justly
wielded rod, there dharma stands firm forever, and the law of the fish
never takes hold.*

## Translation key (cover until you've tried)

1. In olden times there was no king, no rod of punishment, and no one to
   wield it; all the people protected one another by dharma alone.
2. Then, when dharma was destroyed among men overcome by greed, the
   strong began to kill the weak, as one fish devours another in the
   water — this is the law of the fish.
3. Then all of them, oppressed by one another, approached the god
   Brahmā, source of the world, and with joined palms said:
4. "We are without a protector and we are perishing; we have no one to
   guard us. Therefore appoint for us at once some lord, for our
   protection."
5. Then the blessed god appointed Manu as king, and, being able, he
   created the rod out of a desire for the welfare of all beings.
6. In whatever kingdom the people are protected by a justly wielded rod,
   there dharma stands firm forever, and the law of the fish never takes
   hold.
