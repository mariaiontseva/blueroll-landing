Nyāyamañjarī · Āhnika V · practice passage (Vol II 48-49)

English translation with Sanskrit terms in parentheses; brief Russian commentary under each block. Pure prose; ≈ half a printed page.
Подобран как близнец двух твоих отрывков, но **без стихов** и в более доступной форме. Опровергается *ākṛti-вāда* — мīмāṃсака-теория, что значение слова «корова» — это её внешняя конфигурация. Та же лексика (śabda, padārtha, vyakti, jāti, pratyakṣa), знаменитый «корова-пример», классический ход *‘yad uktam — tad ayuktam’*, далее три ablative-довода.

Translation

Even ordinary people (loka) call the form (ākṛti) ‘the arrangement of parts’ (avayava-sanniveśa): as the saying goes, ‘where there is a form, there qualities reside’ (yatrākṛtis tatra guṇā vasanti). So **form = the arrangement of parts**.

Зачин — словарное определение, подкреплённое поговоркой. *Sanniveśa-ātmikā* «состоящая в расположении» (sanniveśa + ātmikā, sandhi a+ā=ā).

Is form (ākṛti) really the meaning of a word or not? — This is a real question, worth investigating; we do not, like the Mīmāṃsakas (jaiminīya), dismiss it as obvious. Since the word ‘cow’ (gauḥ) is used when an individual (vyakti), a form (ākṛti) and a universal (jāti) are all present at once, **which of them is its referent?** — that is the question.

Классификационный поворот: три кандидата на «значение слова» — vyakti, ākṛti, jāti. Этот треугольник проходит сквозь весь раздел II.47-63.

The form-theorists (ākṛti-vādin) reply first: ‘a word’s meaning is fixed by two things — its **use** (prayoga) and the **understanding** it produces (pratipatti). Whatever object the competent (buddhāḥ) apply the word ‘cow’ to in their own usage, and whatever object hearers (śrotāraḥ) grasp from it — **that** is its meaning.’

Излагается **pūrvapakṣa**. Двойной критерий *prayoga + pratipatti* — общее место и в Mīmāṃsā, и в Nyāya.

Now: the word ‘cow’ is **not** used for a maned animal (kesarādimat — i.e. the lion), but it **is** used for a dewlap-bearing animal (sāsnādimat — the cow). So it is understood to refer to **that unique configuration of parts** (asādhāraṇa-sanniveśa) [shared by all cows but no other animal].

Знаменитый «корова-пример». *Kesarādimati / sāsnādimati* — loc. sg. от bahuvrīhi «имеющий гриву / подгрудок».

The word ‘cow’ and similar words are applied to **what is perceptible** (pratyakṣa-viṣaya); and perception itself takes the form (ākṛti) as its object. After all, the configuration of a cow’s body (go-piṇḍa-sanniveśa) is grasped **by the sense organ itself** as different from the configuration of a horse’s body (aśva-piṇḍa-sanniveśa). The **mutual differentiation of things** (itaretara-vailakṣaṇyam) is caused precisely by that configuration.

Мост к Āhnika 2 — ākṛti-вāдин апеллирует к pratyakṣa. *Indriyeṇa* (instr.) подчёркнуто: «самим органом чувств».

‘So a word that operates in the field of perception must operate **precisely upon the form** (ākṛtau eva). And its connection with actions like sending an animal off (preṣaṇa) and so on will hold for the form **by way of the individual** (vyakti-dvāraka).’ — **So they have argued; but this is wrong** (tad ayuktam).

Завершение pūrvapakṣa и стандартный мост *yad uktam — tad ayuktam*: «сказанное — неверно». Дальше — три довода Джаянты.

[Refutation, three arguments:] (i) because a **different configuration is seen in each individual** (prati-vyakti bhinna-saṃsthāna-darśanāt); (ii) because the word–form connection cannot be established — both because there is **no end to individuals** (ānantya) and because there are **exceptions** (vyabhicāra); (iii) the word ‘cow’ does **not** denote any one fixed configuration — e.g. the Śābaleya (spotted-cow) configuration — because even when that configuration is absent we see it used for, e.g., the Bāhuleya (red cow).

Три ablative-причины (*-darśanāt, -asambhavāt, -darśanāt*) — типичная naiyāyika-«арифметика». Śābaleya и Bāhuleya — пятнистая и рыжая коровы, классические примеры.
