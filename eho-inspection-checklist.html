EHO inspection: what to expect and how to be ready

What happens when the environmental health officer walks in, how often they come, the three scores behind your food hygiene rating, a checklist to work through, and what to do after the visit.

Checked 18 November 2026 against the Food Law Code of Practice (England, issued October 2025), the Food Hygiene Rating Scheme Brand Standard and FSA guidance on GOV.UK. Sources are at the bottom of the page.
In short

An EHO inspection is a visit from your council's food safety officer, usually without warning. The officer checks how food is handled, the condition of the building and how you manage food safety, scores each of the three, and gives you a food hygiene rating from 0 to 5 in writing, on the day or within 14 days. In England, how often they come depends on your risk category: from at least every 6 months for the highest risk to at least every 36 months for the lowest.

What happens on an EHO visit

The officer works to the Food Law Code of Practice, which local councils must follow. This is what to expect.

  1. They arrive, usually unannouncedInspections are carried out without prior notice unless notice is needed for the check to work. The officer has a legal right to enter food premises at all reasonable hours and will show you their ID if you ask.
  2. They watch how food is handledHow food is prepared, cooked, reheated, cooled and stored, how temperatures are controlled and whether raw and ready-to-eat food are kept apart. They may take food samples and photographs.
  3. They look at the buildingCleanliness, layout, the condition of the structure, lighting, ventilation, handwashing and other facilities, and pest control.
  4. They check how you manage food safetyYour food safety management system (Safer Food, Better Business or your own HACCP plan), the records that show it working, and staff training. They also weigh your track record, whether you acted on advice from last time and how well you and your team understand the risks.
  5. You get your rating in writingEither at the end of the visit or within 14 days, weekends and bank holidays included. The letter shows the score for each area and, below a 5, what you must do to comply.
Your team is part of the inspection. Confidence in management looks at whether staff are properly supervised, instructed and trained. Make sure whoever is on shift knows where the records are and can explain the basics: handwashing, fridge temperatures and what to do when a customer asks about allergens.

How often EHO inspections happen

In England the gap between inspections depends on your risk category, from A (most often) to E (least often). The Food Law Code of Practice sets the minimum for each.

CategoryRisk scoreInspected at least
A92 or moreEvery 6 months
B72 to 91Every 12 months
C52 to 71Every 18 months
D31 to 51Every 24 months
E0 to 30Every 36 months

The risk score adds up what you do (the type of food and how it is handled, how many people you serve, and whether they include vulnerable groups such as children under 5 or people over 65) and how you did at your last inspection (your three scores). Better inspection scores lower the total, so a good inspection can mean fewer visits.

New businesses

Register with your council at least 28 days before you start trading. If you prepare open high-risk food for 20 or more meals a day, as most restaurants do, the council should carry out its first inspection within 28 days of registration, or of finding out you are trading. Lower-hazard businesses can wait up to three months. A new owner taking over an existing business is treated the same way.

When they come sooner

The council can bring an inspection forward when information suggests something has changed, such as a complaint. Businesses in a recognised assurance scheme may be inspected less often. Wales and Northern Ireland have their own codes of practice, and Scotland has its own scheme (see Scotland below).

Your food hygiene rating comes from three scores. Lower is better: 0 means the officer found nothing to fix.

One weak area caps your rating. Scores of 5, 5 and 5 add up to 15 and give a 5. Scores of 5, 5 and 20 add up to 30, but the 20 pulls the rating down to a 1. Confidence in management has no 15: it goes from 10 straight to 20, so weak management and records alone can mean a 1, and a 25 or 30 in any area means a 0.

Confidence in management is the officer's judgement on whether you will keep standards up after they leave. The Code lists what shapes it: your track record and complaint history, whether you acted on earlier advice, the attitude of management, food safety knowledge including HACCP, and food safety management procedures that suit the size and type of your business.

The checks follow the three scores. Tick what is true in your kitchen today and the bar shows how ready you are. Nothing you tick leaves this page.

Area 1Food handling

Fridges at 5°C or below. By law chilled food must be at 8°C or below; setting fridges to 5°C gives you a margin. Probe one and compare it with today's log.
Freezers at around -18°C, with nothing thawed and refrozen.
Raw meat, fish and poultry kept apart from ready-to-eat food: a separate fridge, or raw food stored below.
Separate chopping boards and utensils for raw food, and staff actually use them. Colour coding helps.
Cooking checked with a clean probe in the centre. Standard advice is 70°C for 2 minutes, or 75°C for 30 seconds.
Hot food held at 63°C or above. Below 63°C for no more than 2 hours, then cooled to 8°C or below or thrown away.
Reheated food steaming hot all the way through, and reheated only once.
Hot food cooled quickly before it goes into the fridge.
Food covered, labelled and in date, nothing past its use-by date, older stock used first.
Defrosting in the fridge, not on the counter.
Hands washed with soap and warm water between tasks and after handling raw food.
Clean workwear, hair tied back, cuts covered, and staff know to report illness before a shift.
Allergen information for every dish on today's menu, and staff know where to find it.

How to run a mock EHO inspection

A mock inspection is you, or someone from outside the kitchen, walking through the business the way the officer would, before the officer does. Do it on an ordinary busy day, not straight after a deep clean.

  1. Pick an ordinary service and don't warn anyoneReal inspections usually come without notice, so tidying up first defeats the point.
  2. Walk the kitchen with the checklist aboveHandwash basin, fridges, storage, prep areas, bins and the back door. Probe a fridge and compare it with today's log.
  3. Watch food being handled for ten minutesHandwashing between tasks, separate boards for raw meat, labels and dates on everything that has been opened.
  4. Ask whoever is on shift three questionsWhat should this fridge read? What do you do if a customer asks about allergens? What happens if a delivery arrives too warm?
  5. Pull the last four weeks of recordsLook for gaps, pages filled in afterwards in the same pen, and problems with no corrective action written down.
  6. Score yourself honestly in the three areasUse the table above to see your likely rating, then fix the weakest area first, because it sets the ceiling.

After an EHO inspection

What arrives, when your rating goes public, and what you can do if you disagree with it.

The letter

You get your rating in writing, on the day or within 14 days. The letter should include:

  • your rating and the score for each of the three areas
  • why you were rated that way and, below a 5, what you must do in each area to comply with the law
  • when the rating will be published and when you will get your sticker
  • how to appeal, the deadline, and contact details for the inspecting officer
  • whether your council charges for a re-rating visit, and how much

The officer must make clear which points are legal requirements and which are good-practice recommendations. If something is unclear, call the inspecting officer. Talking it through may settle it, but it does not stop the clock on the appeal deadline.

When your rating goes online

A 5 is published on the FSA ratings website as soon as your council uploads it. Ratings of 0 to 4 go online 3 to 5 weeks after the inspection, to leave time for an appeal. You can ask the council to publish a new rating before the appeal period ends. In Wales and Northern Ireland you must display your sticker at or near each customer entrance; in England displaying it is voluntary.

If there are serious problems

The officer can serve a Hygiene Improvement Notice, or a Hygiene Emergency Prohibition Notice that stops you using certain equipment or processes. Either way, they will come back to check you have done what the notice requires.

Asking for a re-rating visit

Write to your council (email is fine) with what you have fixed since the inspection and your evidence, for example photos, invoices, training certificates and your records since the visit. The council can refuse a request without enough evidence. At the visit the officer looks at standards generally, not just the points you fixed.

  • The fee. Some councils in England charge for a re-rating visit, and every council in Wales and Northern Ireland does. See what councils charge and get a free request letter.
  • Timing in England. If your council charges, the visit should happen within three months of your request or payment, and there is no limit on requests. If it does not charge, the visit will not usually happen in the first three months after the inspection, should happen within three months after that, and you get one requested visit between routine inspections.
  • Timing in Wales and Northern Ireland. The visit happens within three months of your written request, your current sticker must be on display, and there is no limit on requests.

Scotland

Scotland runs the Food Hygiene Information Scheme instead. The result is Pass or Improvement Required. You can appeal within 14 days and add a right to reply, and with Improvement Required you can ask for one revisit between routine inspections. Revisits are free at the moment.

Questions owners ask

Are EHO inspections announced?

Usually not. The Food Law Code of Practice says inspections are carried out without prior notice unless notice is needed for the check to work, so plan to be ready on any day.

Can I refuse an EHO inspection?

No. Under section 32 of the Food Safety Act 1990 an authorised officer can enter food premises at all reasonable hours. Intentionally obstructing them, or refusing without reasonable cause the help or information they need, is an offence under section 33. You can ask to see their ID.

How often does the EHO inspect a restaurant?

In England it depends on your risk category: at least every 6 months for category A, then every 12, 18, 24 and 36 months for categories B to E. A new business preparing open high-risk food for 20 or more meals a day should be inspected within 28 days of registering.

What documents should I have ready for an EHO inspection?

Your food safety management system (Safer Food, Better Business or a HACCP plan), daily opening and closing checks, temperature records, your cleaning schedule, corrective actions, staff training records, supplier details, pest control reports and allergen information for every dish.

What happens after an EHO inspection?

You get your rating in writing on the day or within 14 days, with the score for each area and what to fix. A 5 goes online as soon as the council uploads it. Ratings of 0 to 4 are published 3 to 5 weeks after the inspection, so you have time to appeal.

How do I appeal my food hygiene rating?

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, write to your council's lead officer for food within 21 days of being told your rating. The officer who inspected you cannot decide the appeal, and you should get the decision within 21 days. In Scotland the deadline is 14 days.

How much does a food hygiene re-rating cost?

It depends where you are. Some councils in England charge and every council in Wales and Northern Ireland does. A revisit in Scotland is free at the moment. Our re-rating guide lists fees council by council.

What is a mock EHO inspection?

A practice inspection you run yourself, or ask someone from outside the kitchen to run, using the same three areas the officer scores. It shows you what to fix before the real visit. Our free self-audits walk you through one.
