How London food hygiene ratings work

Every food business in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland is given a food hygiene rating from 0 (urgent improvement necessary) to 5 (very good) under the Food Hygiene Rating Scheme (FHRS), run by the Food Standards Agency in partnership with local authorities.

In London, inspections are carried out by Environmental Health Officers (EHOs) employed by your local borough council — Westminster, Camden, Tower Hamlets, and so on. There are 32 London boroughs plus the City of London Corporation, each with its own EHO team.

Your rating is based on three scoring areas. Each area is scored, and the scores are combined to produce your overall rating. The lower your total score, the higher your rating — it is a penalty-point system, not a reward system.

Your rating is public. It appears on the FSA website, on Just Eat, Deliveroo, Uber Eats, and Google. In England, displaying the green sticker is technically voluntary — but customers check online. A rating below 4 directly impacts footfall, delivery orders, and revenue.

The inspection process

Unannounced visits

EHO inspectors arrive without notice during normal working hours. The inspection typically takes 1-3 hours depending on the size and complexity of your business. They will inspect every area where food is stored, prepared, cooked, or served — kitchens, walk-in fridges, dry stores, prep areas, and front of house.

What inspectors check

The inspector assesses your business against three areas, each scored separately:

  • Hygiene: How food is handled, prepared, cooked, cooled, reheated, and stored. Cross-contamination controls. Personal hygiene of staff.
  • Structure: The physical condition of the building, equipment, cleanliness, layout, lighting, ventilation, and pest control.
  • Confidence in management: Your food safety management system (e.g., SFBB), staff training records, track record of compliance, and whether you can demonstrate ongoing control.

After the inspection

You receive a written report detailing any contraventions found and the improvements required. Your rating is published on the FSA website, usually within a few days. If you are unhappy with your rating, you can appeal within 21 days or request a re-inspection after making improvements (typically after a minimum 3-month wait).

The three scoring areas

Each scoring area uses a points system. Lower points are better. The three area scores are combined to produce your overall rating.

Scoring areaPoint rangeWhat it covers
Hygiene0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25Food handling, preparation, cooking, cooling, cross-contamination, personal hygiene
Structure0, 5, 10, 15, 20Cleanliness, condition of building, layout, lighting, ventilation, pest control facilities
Confidence in management0, 5, 10, 20, 30Food safety procedures (SFBB/HACCP), staff training, compliance history, ability to maintain standards

Management confidence is the swing factor. A score of 0 in management (excellent) versus 20 (poor) is the difference between a rating of 5 and a rating of 2 — even if your hygiene and structure scores are identical. Inspectors want to see documented systems that prove you are in control.

How to get and keep a rating of 5

Most restaurants that drop from a 5 do so because of inconsistent daily practices, not one-off disasters. The key is systematic daily monitoring and documentation.

Temperature control

  • Check fridge temperatures at least twice daily — must be 8°C or below (ideally 5°C or below)
  • Check freezer temperature daily — must be -18°C or below
  • Hot food must be held at 63°C or above
  • Probe-check cooking temperatures for every batch — poultry core 75°C
  • Record all temperatures with time, date, and initials
  • Cool cooked food to below 8°C within 90 minutes, then refrigerate

Hand washing

  • Dedicated hand wash basin in every food preparation area — not used for food or dishes
  • Hot and cold running water, antibacterial soap, and disposable paper towels
  • Hand wash sign posted at every basin
  • Check and restock soap and towels at opening, mid-service, and close

Cross-contamination

  • Raw meat and fish stored separately from ready-to-eat food (raw below, cooked above)
  • Colour-coded chopping boards used consistently
  • Separate utensils for raw and cooked food
  • Allergen information documented for every dish and available to customers

Documentation (management confidence)

  • SFBB pack or equivalent HACCP-based system completed and up to date
  • Daily temperature logs, opening/closing checks, and cleaning schedules recorded
  • Staff food hygiene training records on file (Level 2 minimum for food handlers)
  • Pest control contract in place with records of visits
  • Corrective actions documented when something goes wrong
  • Supplier records and traceability information kept for at least 3 months

Frequently asked questions

How do food hygiene ratings work in London?

Environmental Health Officers from your local borough council inspect three areas: food hygiene and safety, structural compliance, and confidence in management. Each area is scored using a penalty-point system. Your total score determines your rating from 0 (urgent improvement) to 5 (very good). You need 15 points or fewer to achieve a 5.

How often are London restaurants inspected?

Inspection frequency depends on your risk level and previous rating. High-risk businesses like restaurants and takeaways are typically inspected every 6-12 months. Lower-risk businesses may be inspected every 2-3 years. A business with a rating of 0 or 1 will be re-inspected within 6 months. All inspections are unannounced.

Do I have to display my food hygiene rating?

In England, display is voluntary — though the FSA strongly encourages it and many customers expect it. In Wales and Northern Ireland, display is mandatory by law. Regardless, your rating is visible on the FSA website, Google, and most food delivery platforms.

Can I appeal or request a re-inspection?

Yes. You have 21 days from receiving your rating to lodge a formal appeal with the local authority. You can also request a re-inspection after making improvements, though most boroughs require a minimum 3-month wait and may charge a fee (typically around £150-£200).

What is the most common reason for a low rating?

Lack of a documented food safety management system. If you have no SFBB pack (or HACCP-based equivalent) in place, you will almost certainly score poorly in the management category — which is the single biggest factor in your overall rating. Temperature control and cleanliness are the next most common issues.

What is SFBB and do I need it?

Safer Food Better Business (SFBB) is the FSA's free food safety management system designed for small food businesses. It is not strictly mandatory — the law requires a documented system based on HACCP principles, and SFBB satisfies this requirement. You can use SFBB, a commercial alternative like Blueroll, or your own HACCP plan. What matters is that you have a system, it is documented, and your team follows it.
