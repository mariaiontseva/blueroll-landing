How to get a 5 star food hygiene rating

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland the inspector scores three things, and the worst of the three sets your ceiling. A spotless kitchen with patchy records still can't get a 5. Your three scores are on the letter the inspector sent after the visit.

The three things an inspector scores

Each area gets its own score, in fixed steps. Lower is better: 0 means very good, 25 or 30 means urgent improvement. For a 5, every area has to be at 0 or 5.

If you're on a 4

Find the area scored 10 on your inspection letter. The letter also lists what the inspector wants changed.

Once these are fixed and your records show it, you can ask for a re-rating. A 4 qualifies: the re-visit scheme covers every rating from 0 to 4. What it costs and how to ask.

  • An SFBB or HACCP pack that no longer matches how the kitchen works, for example after a menu change.
  • Records with gaps, or pages that look filled in all at once.
  • Points from the last inspection letter that are still not done.
  • New starters who haven't been shown your system.
  • Only one person who can explain the system. If they're off on the day, nobody else can answer the inspector.

What to do before your next inspection

In the order that usually moves the score most.

  1. Get your written system in place and in useSafer Food, Better Business or your own HACCP plan, filled in for your kitchen, not a blank pack on a shelf.
  2. Keep the daily records every dayFridge and freezer temperatures, cooking and cooling checks, opening and closing checks, cleaning sign-offs. Inspectors look for gaps and for weeks filled in with one pen.
  3. Make allergens answerableEvery dish with its 14 allergens, kept up to date when a recipe changes, so any member of staff can answer a customer.
  4. Show your team is trainedFood hygiene certificates and a record of who was shown what, including new starters.
  5. Fix the building items on your last letterRepairs, handwash basins, pest proofing. Keep invoices and photos: you will need them if you ask for a re-rating.

The temperatures an inspector checks

Temperature control is part of the food handling score. These are the figures in the government's food hygiene guidance for businesses.

Hot food

70°C

Cooking: in the centre for 2 minutes.

75°C

Also safe for cooking: 30 seconds at 75°C, or 6 seconds at 80°C.

63°C

Hot holding: 63°C or above. Hot food on display below 63°C: up to 2 hours, then cool it to 8°C or below, or throw it away.

Once

Reheating: steaming hot all the way through, and only once.

Cold food

8°C

Chilled food: 8°C or below, a legal requirement in England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

5°C

Fridges: set to 5°C or below so food stays cold enough.

4 hours

Chilled food out on display: up to 4 hours, then throw it away.

Re-rating: what the visit costs and how to ask

You don't have to wait for the next routine inspection. Once you've fixed what the inspector found, you can ask your council to come back and rate you again.

England

£120to£371

Each council decides whether to charge and how much. This is what the ones we checked charge.

Wales

about £255

Every council charges.

Northern Ireland

£150

Every council charges.

How soon the visit happens

WhereWhen
Where the council chargesWithin three months of your request, or of your payment if it wants the fee first.
England, no chargeUsually not in the first three months after the inspection, and you shouldn't wait more than six months from asking.
Wales and Northern IrelandWithin three months of your written request.

How to ask

  1. 1

    Accept the rating. If you think it was wrong on the day, appeal in writing within 21 days instead.

  2. 2

    Fix every point on the inspector's letter, in all three areas. Keep photos, invoices and certificates as you go.

  3. 3

    Write to the council. Email is fine. Say what you changed and attach the evidence, including your records since the inspection. A council can turn down a request without enough evidence.

  4. 4

    Pay the fee if your council charges one.

  5. 5

    The inspector comes back unannounced and looks at everything, not just what you fixed. The rating can go up, down or stay the same.

If your fixes are mostly records and management, a council that charges may time the visit towards the end of the three months, to see that the change has lasted. Keep the daily records going from the day you ask.

Re-rating fees council by council, appeal or re-rating, and a request letter you can copy.

Questions owners ask

What do you need for a 5 food hygiene rating?

All three area scores at 0 or 5, adding up to 15 or less. One weak area caps the rating however good the others are.

Is a food hygiene rating of 4 good?

Yes. A 4 means hygiene standards are good, one step below the top rating. What stops it being a 5 is at least one area scored 10 instead of 5, and once that's fixed you can ask for a re-rating visit.

What's the difference between a 4 and a 5 food hygiene rating?

For a 5, all three area scores must be 0 or 5, which keeps the total at 15 or less. A 4 allows areas at 10 and a total of up to 20. So scores of 0, 5 and 10 add up to 15 but still give a 4.

What is confidence in management?

The inspector's view of how well you manage food safety: a written system such as SFBB or HACCP, records that are kept, staff training and your track record. With no written system this area scores 20 or 30, which on its own holds the rating at 1 or 0.

Can I get my food hygiene rating changed before the next inspection?

Yes. Accept the rating, fix everything on the inspector's letter, then ask your council in writing for a re-rating visit. The visit is unannounced and the rating can go up, down or stay the same. Full guide.

How much does a food hygiene re-rating visit cost?

In England each council decides whether to charge and how much. The ones we checked charge £120 to £371. In Wales every council charges, about £255, and in Northern Ireland it's £150. Fees council by council.

What temperature should a fridge be for a food hygiene inspection?

Chilled food must be kept at 8°C or below in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Guidance is to set fridges to 5°C or below so food stays cold enough when the temperature changes. Write the readings down so the inspector can see them.

Do I have to display my food hygiene rating?

In Wales and Northern Ireland, yes, at each customer entrance. In England it's voluntary, but your rating is published online either way.
