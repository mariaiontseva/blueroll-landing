How to run a self-inspection

  1. Pick a busy time. Problems show up during service.
  2. Walk the kitchen in the inspector’s order: handwashing sink, coolers, hot line, prep, dish area, storage, restrooms.
  3. Probe three items in every cooler and on the hot line, and write the numbers down.
  4. Mark each line yes or no. For every no, write the fix, who owns it and by when.
  5. Repeat weekly and keep the sheets with your corrective action log.

The food temperature log and the restaurant cleaning checklist are the daily records behind most of these checks.

What an inspector checks first

Inspectors start with the FDA’s five risk factors for foodborne illness. Violations here usually cost the most points.

Food from unsafe sources

Approved suppliers, invoices kept, shellfish tags kept.

Inadequate cooking

Final cooking temperatures probed and written down.

Improper holding temperatures

Cold food at 41°F or below, hot food at 135°F or above, cooling logged.

Contaminated equipment

Food-contact surfaces cleaned and sanitized, sanitizer at the right strength.

Poor personal hygiene

Handwashing, no bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat food, an ill-worker policy staff know.

Frequently asked questions

What do health inspectors look for in a restaurant?

Food temperatures, handwashing and bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat food, how food is stored and labeled, whether sanitizer is at the right strength, signs of pests, and paperwork such as your permit and a Certified Food Protection Manager. The checklist covers each of these.

How often should I do a self-inspection?

Weekly works for most restaurants, plus one more when an inspection is due. Keep the completed sheets with your corrective action log.
