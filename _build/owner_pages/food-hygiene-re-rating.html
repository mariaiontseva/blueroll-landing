Got a 1, 2 or 3? How to get re-rated

You don't have to wait for the next routine inspection. Fix what the inspector found, then ask your council for a re-rating visit. Here's what it costs where you are, how long it takes, and a request you can send today.

What it costs and how long it takes

How a re-rating works

  1. Accept your ratingIf you think the rating was wrong on the day, appeal in writing within 21 days instead. You can't appeal and ask for a re-rating at the same time.
  2. Fix everything on the inspector's letterEvery point, in all three areas. Keep photos, invoices and certificates as you go.
  3. Keep records for a few weeksInspectors want to see the change has stuck: daily temperatures, cleaning sign-offs and checks, filled in on the day.
  4. Send your requestIn writing, to the officer named on your rating letter or through your council's online form. Use the letter below.
  5. Pay the fee if your council chargesMany councils take payment by phone once they've checked your request.
  6. The inspector comes back unannouncedThe new rating follows at the visit or within 14 days, and replaces the old one online.
The rating can go down as well as up. The re-rating visit is a full inspection. Only ask once the fixes are in place and your records show them working.

Questions owners ask

How much does a re-rating cost?

England: most councils charge, usually £210 to £350; we found £120 to £371. Wales: about £255. Northern Ireland: £150. Scotland: a revisit is currently free.

How long until the inspector comes back?

Usually within three months of your request. In England, if your council doesn't charge, there's a three-month wait after the inspection first. The new rating follows within 14 days of the visit.

Can I ask more than once?

In England, if your council charges there's no limit; if it doesn't, you get one re-visit per routine inspection. In Scotland it's one revisit between planned inspections.

What should I attach?

Photos of the work, invoices and receipts, copies of your daily records since the inspection, training certificates and your latest pest control report. Councils can refuse a request without enough evidence.

How do I get to a 5 rather than a 4?

All three areas have to score 0 or 5. Work out what's holding you back.
