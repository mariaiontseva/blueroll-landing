How to get a 5 food hygiene rating

The inspector scores three things, and the worst of the three sets your ceiling. A spotless kitchen with patchy records still can't get a 5. Pick your scores below and see exactly what holds you back.

Based on the Food Standards Agency scoring used in England, Wales and Northern Ireland · checked {{UPDATED}}

Work out your rating

Your scores are on the letter the inspector sent after the visit. Lower is better: 0 means very good, 25 or 30 means urgent improvement.

The three things an inspector scores

Each area gets its own score. For a 5, every one of them has to be 0 or 5.

Area 1

Food handling

How food is stored, prepared, cooked, cooled and reheated. Temperatures, cross-contamination, handwashing.

Area 2

Building and cleaning

Cleanliness, repair, layout, lighting, ventilation, pest control and handwashing facilities.

Area 3 · the one paper loses

Food safety management

A written system such as SFBB or HACCP, records that are actually filled in, staff training and your track record.

Why so many kitchens stop at a 3 or 4

The rating isn't just the total. Each rating also has a ceiling on the worst single area, so one weak area caps you however good the rest is.

Food safety management is where this bites hardest. Inspectors give it 10 when a written system is in progress, 5 when it is in place, and 20 or 30 when there is none. A score of 20 holds the whole rating at 1, even with a perfect kitchen. It is also the area you can fix fastest, because it is mostly paperwork that has to exist and be kept up every day.

What to do before your next inspection

In the order that usually moves the score most.

  1. Get your written system in place and in useSafer Food, Better Business or your own HACCP plan, filled in for your kitchen, not a blank pack on a shelf.
  2. Keep the daily records every dayFridge and freezer temperatures, cooking and cooling checks, opening and closing checks, cleaning sign-offs. Inspectors look for gaps and for weeks filled in with one pen.
  3. Make allergens answerableEvery dish with its 14 allergens, kept up to date when a recipe changes, so any member of staff can answer a customer.
  4. Show your team is trainedFood hygiene certificates and a record of who was shown what, including new starters.
  5. Fix the building items on your last letterRepairs, handwash basins, pest proofing. Keep invoices and photos: you will need them if you ask for a re-rating.

A quick self-check

  • Fridge and freezer temperatures recorded at least twice a dayWith the time and who checked, and what was done if one was out of range.
  • Cooking, cooling and reheating temperatures checked and written down
  • Cleaning schedule signed off every day
  • Your SFBB or HACCP pack is completed for your own menu and processes
  • Allergen information for every dish, up to date
  • Training certificates on file for everyone who handles food
  • Pest control contract or checks, with the latest report
  • Supplier details and delivery checks recorded

Questions owners ask

What do you need for a 5 food hygiene rating?

All three area scores at 0 or 5, adding up to 15 or less. One weak area caps the rating however good the others are.

What is confidence in management?

The inspector's view of how well you manage food safety: a written system such as SFBB or HACCP, records that are kept, staff training and your track record. With no written system this area scores 20 or 30, which on its own holds the rating at 1 or 0.

Can I get my food hygiene rating changed before the next inspection?

Yes. Accept the rating, make the improvements, then ask your council for a re-rating visit. Most councils in England charge a fee, Wales about £255 and Northern Ireland £150. The visit is unannounced and the rating can go up, down or stay the same. Full guide.

Do I have to display my food hygiene rating?

In Wales and Northern Ireland, yes, at each customer entrance. In England it's voluntary, but your rating is published online either way.
