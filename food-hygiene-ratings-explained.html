FHRS Guide

Food hygiene ratings explained

What ratings 0 to 5 actually mean, how inspections work, the three scoring criteria, and when display is a legal requirement.

5
Very good
The highest rating. The business meets all food hygiene requirements and follows best practices. All three scoring areas are performing well. Approximately 70% of UK food businesses hold a rating of 5.
4
Good
Hygiene standards are good overall, but there are some minor areas for improvement. The business may have small issues in one scoring area — perhaps a few missing records or a minor maintenance task. Nothing that poses a risk to food safety.
3
Generally satisfactory
Hygiene standards are acceptable but improvement is needed. This often indicates that while food handling is adequate, documentation and management procedures need strengthening. Many businesses at this level have the practices in place but are not recording them consistently.
2
Improvement necessary
Significant issues were found during the inspection. The business needs to make real improvements to food handling, building condition, or food safety management. The local authority will have outlined the specific areas requiring attention and will expect action before the next inspection.
1
Major improvement necessary
Serious problems with food hygiene were found. The business has significant failings in one or more scoring areas. The local authority may issue a formal written warning or take other enforcement action. Substantial changes are needed.
0
Urgent improvement necessary
The most serious outcome. Conditions at the business may pose an imminent risk to public health. The local authority is likely to take enforcement action, which could include issuing a Hygiene Emergency Prohibition Notice to close the business until the problems are resolved.

The three scoring criteria

During an inspection, Environmental Health Officers assess three distinct areas. Each area is scored independently, and the scores combine to determine the overall 0-5 rating. Understanding these areas is essential for any food business that wants to maintain or improve its rating.

1. Hygienic food handling

This criterion covers how food is prepared, cooked, reheated, cooled, and stored. Inspectors observe whether cross-contamination is being prevented, whether cooking and cooling temperatures are correct, and whether allergens are properly managed. They will watch staff at work and check that practices match documented procedures. Key observations include use of colour-coded equipment, correct fridge temperatures and storage order, and personal hygiene standards.

2. Cleanliness and condition of facilities and building

The structural assessment examines the physical state of the premises. This includes the cleanliness of all surfaces and equipment, the layout of the kitchen, the condition of walls, floors, and ceilings, the availability of hand wash basins, ventilation, lighting, and pest control measures. A kitchen might handle food well but lose marks here if the building is poorly maintained — for example, cracked tiles that harbour bacteria or a lack of adequate hand-washing facilities.

3. Management of food safety

This is where inspectors assess whether the business has a documented food safety management system (based on HACCP principles) and whether it is actually being used. They want to see completed daily and weekly record sheets, staff training records, temperature logs, cleaning schedules, and evidence that the system is reviewed and updated. This area is consistently where businesses lose the most marks, because it requires ongoing documentation rather than a one-time setup.

How inspections work

Food hygiene inspections in the UK are carried out by Environmental Health Officers (EHOs) employed by local authorities. Here is how the process typically works:

Unannounced visits

Inspections are almost always unannounced. The EHO will arrive during normal business hours and identify themselves. You cannot refuse an inspection — EHOs have a legal right of entry to food premises. The inspection usually takes between one and three hours, depending on the size and complexity of the business.

What happens during an inspection

The inspector will typically start by walking through the premises, observing food handling practices and assessing the physical condition of the kitchen. They will check temperatures of fridges, freezers, and hot-holding equipment. They will ask to see your food safety management records, training certificates, cleaning schedules, and pest control reports. They may ask staff questions about food safety procedures to verify that training has been effective.

After the inspection

The EHO will discuss their findings with you before leaving and will explain any issues found. You will later receive a written report detailing the scores for each area and any required or recommended actions. Your rating is then published on the FSA website. Businesses have a right to appeal the rating if they believe it is unfair, and in some cases can request a re-inspection after improvements have been made.

Inspection frequency

How often your business is inspected depends on its risk profile. Newly registered food businesses are usually inspected within 28 days. After that, high-risk businesses (those with previous low ratings or complex operations) may be inspected every 6 months. Medium-risk businesses are typically inspected every 12-18 months. Low-risk businesses with consistently high ratings may only be visited every 2-3 years.

Scotland operates differently. Scotland uses the Food Hygiene Information Scheme (FHIS), which gives a simple Pass or Improvement Required result, rather than a 0-5 numeric rating. The inspection process is similar, but the published outcome is binary rather than graded.

Legal requirements for displaying ratings

The rules around displaying food hygiene ratings vary depending on where your business is located within the UK:

Wales

Under the Food Hygiene Rating (Wales) Act 2013, all food businesses in Wales are legally required to display their rating sticker at or near the entrance to their premises, in a position where it can be easily read by customers. Failure to display the sticker, or displaying an incorrect rating, can result in a fixed penalty notice. Online food businesses must also display their rating on their website and on third-party platforms where their food is listed.

Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland has similar mandatory display requirements under the Food Hygiene Rating Act (Northern Ireland) 2016. All food businesses must display their rating sticker in a conspicuous place that is readily visible to customers. The rules also apply to online businesses.

England

In England, displaying your food hygiene rating is currently voluntary. However, the FSA strongly encourages all food businesses to display their rating, and many customers actively look for it. There have been ongoing discussions about making display mandatory in England, as it already is in Wales and Northern Ireland. Even without a legal requirement, displaying a good rating builds trust and can be a competitive advantage.

Online display

Whether it is mandatory in your jurisdiction or not, displaying your food hygiene rating online is increasingly important. Customers routinely check ratings before ordering from a new restaurant. Frequently asked questions

Can a business still operate with a 0 rating?

Yes, in most cases. A 0 rating does not automatically mean the business is closed. However, the local authority may take enforcement action such as a Hygiene Improvement Notice (requiring specific improvements within a set timeframe) or, in the most serious cases, a Hygiene Emergency Prohibition Notice which does force immediate closure until the problem is resolved.

How long does it take to get a new rating after inspection?

Typically, the rating is published on the FSA website within a few weeks of the inspection. However, businesses have a 21-day window to appeal or respond before the rating is made public. In practice, most ratings appear within 28 days of the inspection.

Do ratings expire?

Ratings do not technically expire, but they are replaced by a new rating following each subsequent inspection. A rating from several years ago may not reflect current standards. The FSA website shows the date of the last inspection alongside each rating, so customers can judge how recent the information is.

Can I check a business's full inspection history?

The FSA website shows the current rating and the date of the last inspection for each business. Detailed inspection reports are held by the local authority and can usually be obtained through a Freedom of Information request. How can I improve my rating?

Focus on the three scoring areas: food handling, building condition, and food safety management. The most impactful quick wins are usually in management — completing your daily records, logging temperatures, and maintaining training files.
