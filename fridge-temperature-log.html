How to use the log

  1. Tape the sheet to the fridge door.
  2. Read every unit when you open and before you close, from the display or a probe in water kept in the unit.
  3. Write the number, the time and your initials, not a tick.
  4. Circle a reading out of range, write down what you did, and recheck.
  5. Check your probe thermometer in iced water and write down the result.

Safe temperatures in the UK

The numbers an inspector expects to see on your log and your probe.

5°C

Fridges

Set fridges and chilled display to 5°C or below.

8°C

Chilled food, at most

The law in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and the recommendation in Scotland.

−18°C

Freezers

Keep frozen food at −18°C or below.

70°C

Cooking

A core temperature of 70°C for 2 minutes, or 75°C for 30 seconds.

63°C

Hot holding

Keep hot food at 63°C or above.

82°C

Reheating in Scotland

Elsewhere, reheat until steaming hot all the way through, and only once.

A probe should read between −1°C and 1°C in iced water, and between 99°C and 101°C in boiling water.

What the law expects

Chilled food must be kept at 8°C or below in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. The Food Standards Agency recommends setting fridges to 5°C or below to keep that margin through door openings and deliveries.

Safer Food, Better Business asks you to check fridges and chilled display at least once a day, starting with your opening checks. In England, holding food above 8°C, or hot food below 63°C, is an offence, with time-limited exceptions for service and display.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I record fridge temperatures?

At least once a day, and better twice: once when you open and once before you close. Record more often for a fridge that is opened all through service or has given trouble. Write the actual number each time, not a tick.

What temperature should a commercial fridge be in the UK?

5°C or below. The legal maximum for chilled food in England, Wales and Northern Ireland is 8°C, so 5°C leaves a margin for door openings and deliveries. Freezers should be at −18°C or below.

What should I do when a fridge reading is too high?

Check again with a probe, in the food or in water kept in the unit, and look for the cause: a door left open, a delivery just loaded, a blocked fan. If the unit stays above 5°C, move the food to another fridge and call an engineer. If you cannot show the food stayed safe, throw it away, and write down the reading, the time, what you did and the recheck.
