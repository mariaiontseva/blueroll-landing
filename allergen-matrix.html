How to fill in your allergen matrix

  1. List everything you serve, including sauces, dressings, garnishes and sides.
  2. Work from labels and recipes, not memory. Bought-in stocks, sauces and bread count too.
  3. Tick each allergen the dish contains. For cereals and tree nuts, name which one in the Notes column.
  4. Use M for may contain, sparingly, only where cross-contact is real and you cannot remove it.
  5. Date it, sign it and put one copy at the pass and one front of house.

The Excel file has the same sheets, with a drop-down in each allergen column and a Contains line you can copy onto your menu.

When to update the matrix

A matrix is only right on the day it was checked. Re-check the row the same day something changes, write it in the change log, then re-date, sign and replace the copies.

A recipe changes

A chef tweaks a dish or a special goes on.

A supplier swaps a brand

Or sends a substitute. Read the label on the delivery, not last month’s.

A product is reformulated

Ask your suppliers to tell you about any allergen changes.

A last-minute substitution

Keep them to a minimum, and check the ingredients when one happens.

The FSA’s manager checklist asks who updates your written allergen information, so decide who that is.

What the law expects

If you sell food that is not prepacked, you must give allergen information for every item that contains any of the 14 allergens. You can give it in writing, or verbally with a clear written notice that tells customers how to ask.

The FSA puts it plainly: accurate allergen information “is a legal requirement”. Local authorities enforce it, with improvement notices, penalties and in some cases prosecution. This guidance applies to England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Food made to order

Give accurate allergen information for every dish, in writing or verbally with a written notice. A matrix is one of the written formats the FSA names.

Prepacked for direct sale (PPDS)

Since 1 October 2021, a label with the name of the food and a full ingredients list, with the 14 allergens emphasised. A matrix does not replace it. More on Natasha’s Law in 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What is an allergen matrix?

A table with every dish down the side and the 14 allergens across the top, with a tick for each allergen a dish contains. Front of house uses it to answer allergen questions from writing instead of memory, and the FSA suggests kitchen staff use one when they plan recipes. It is also one of the ways the FSA suggests giving allergen information to customers in writing.

Is there an official FSA allergen matrix template?

The FSA publishes an example allergen matrix in its best practice guidance for non-prepacked food, and points to an editable version on its allergen icons and posters page. Both are free. This template follows the same layout and adds a dated sign-off, a notes column, a may-contain mark and a change log.

Do I have to use an allergen matrix?

No single format is required. For food that is not prepacked, you must give allergen information for every item that contains any of the 14 allergens, and it must be accurate. You can give it in writing, or verbally with a clearly visible written notice. The FSA's advice is written information supported by a conversation, and a matrix is one of the written formats it names.

Can I mark dishes as free from an allergen?

Not on the matrix. The FSA treats a “free from” claim as a guarantee that needs strict controls. Use M for may contain only where cross-contact is real: the FSA’s example is chips cooked in oil already used for battered fish, which cannot be served as gluten free.

How often should I update my allergen matrix?

Every time something changes that could move a tick: a recipe, an ingredient, a supplier or brand, or a substitution. The FSA also advises checking regularly that your allergen information is still accurate, so date and sign every review.

Can I fill it in on a computer?

Yes. Open the Excel file in Excel or Google Sheets, pick a tick or M from the drop-down in each allergen column, and the last column writes a Contains line you can copy onto your menu. Print it on A4 landscape when you are done.
